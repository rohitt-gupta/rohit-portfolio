My old portfolio did all the things a portfolio is supposed to do. It had a sticky pill navbar, a
scroll-spy that lit up the active section, a vertical timeline for my experience, cards with
screenshots for every project, and a contact form wired up to Resend.

It was fine. It just wasn't very _me_.

## What was wrong with it

Nothing was broken. That was sort of the problem — there was nothing to fix, so I never touched it,
and a site you never touch slowly stops being true.

A few things bothered me every time I opened it:

- **Too many moving parts.** Scroll-spy, intersection observers, a theme context, an active-section
  context. A lot of machinery for what is, honestly, one page of text.
- **The screenshots aged badly.** Project cards with images look great on day one and dated by month
  six.
- **It said very little.** Plenty of sections, not much writing. You could read the whole thing and
  still not know what I actually care about.

## What I wanted instead

One column. Readable text. Enough motion to feel alive and not one frame more.

The rule I set for myself: if a component doesn't help someone understand what I build or how to
reach me, it doesn't ship.

That killed the timeline, the cards, the scroll-spy and the contact form. What's left is a few
paragraphs, a list of things I've built, the stack I reach for, where I've worked, and five ways to
get hold of me.

## What I kept

- **A blog**, because I want somewhere to put things like this.
- **Hover previews on links** — the one bit of flourish I genuinely enjoy.
- **A theme switcher**, tucked in the corner, for anyone who wants a different palette.

If you want to see how any of it is put together, the whole thing is on
[GitHub](https://github.com/rohitt-gupta/rohit-portfolio).
